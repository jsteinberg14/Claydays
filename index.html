Layering tips

  • Put the stiffer glaze on the bottom and the runnier one on top.
  • Keep the top glaze off the lower third of the piece so it has room to flow.
  • Two to three coats of each glaze is the usual starting point.
  • Try a new combo on a test tile or small cup set on a drip tray before glazing a whole batch.
  • Clay color changes the result. Translucent glazes look lighter on white clay and darker on brown or black clay.
  • Cooling matters. Some glazes, like Ancient Copper, change color with a slow cool.

Previews show approximate colors only. AMACO fired these examples on white stoneware, so results on your clay will differ. Browse many more in AMACO’s layering database.

Cone chart

Firing temperatures for each cone, plus a quick temperature converter.

Convert a temperature

Temperatures are for Orton self-supporting cones heated at 108°F (60°C) per hour over the last 200°F of the firing. Firing slower or faster changes when a cone bends, so use witness cones to check. The dot shows roughly how the kiln glows at that heat.
